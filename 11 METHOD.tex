\section{Data and pre-processing}\label{sec:data}

We use high-frequency bid--ask FX tick data from \textcite{dukascopy}, loaded into Python with \texttt{pandas} \parencite{mckinney2010pandas} and \texttt{NumPy} \parencite{harris2020numpy}. The two legs of a pair are indexed by $i \in \{A, B\}$. The three pair constructions are AUD/USD--NZD/USD, EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK, and GBP/USD--EUR/USD; they span a deliberate range of liquidity, transaction costs, and pair-dependence \parencite{gatev2006pairs,krauss2017statarb}.

Rather than running a single continuous back-test, we study three independent one-month sub-samples: \emph{August 2024}, \emph{September 2024}, and \emph{August 2025}. These months were chosen because they cover periods of active price movement on all three pairs while spanning distinct macroeconomic backdrops, which gives three structurally different episodes on which the same machinery can be exercised. Reporting the three episodes side by side is more informative than aggregating them: each month is treated as a self-contained case study, and any difference in conclusions across months is itself a result.

Bid and ask streams are sorted by timestamp and aligned by a backward as-of merge \parencite{hasbrouck2007microstructure}. We write $P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i}$ and $P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i}$ for the prevailing bid and ask of leg $i$ at time $t$. The mid-price and basis-point half-spread are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:midprice}
    P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i} + P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i}\bigr),
    \qquad
    \mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i} - P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i}}{P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}}\times 10^{4}.
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i}$ approximates the cost of crossing the market for one leg \parencite{roll1984spread}. Since pairs trading enters and exits both legs simultaneously, the combined spread cost is central to the back-test.

\subsection{Pre-averaging on number of ticks}\label{sec:preaveraging}

Tick data are dominated by microstructure noise: bid--ask bounce, discreteness, and asynchronous quote updates contaminate the observed price relative to the latent efficient price \parencite{roll1984spread,hasbrouck2007microstructure,hautsch2012econometrics}. Following \textcite{jacod2017}, we therefore apply a \emph{pre-averaging} step in tick time: each block of $L$ consecutive ticks is collapsed into a single observation by averaging the mid-prices within the block. With $\{P_{t_n}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}\}_{n}$ denoting the synchronised tick-time mid-price for leg $i$, the pre-averaged series at block index $k$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preaverage}
    \widetilde P_k^{\,i} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{L}\!\sum_{n=(k-1)L+1}^{kL} P_{t_n}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i},
    \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots
\end{equation}
The block size $L$ is the same for both legs of a pair, so the pre-averaged legs remain perfectly synchronised. Pre-averaging is preferred here to fixed calendar-time sampling because it produces an observation index that is more nearly homogeneous in microstructure activity and substantially reduces the noise variance of the resulting return series \parencite{jacod2017,aitsahalia2014,andersen2000great}. All subsequent quantities---log returns, cointegration spread, $z$-score, and HMM observations---are computed on the pre-averaged series $\widetilde P_k^{\,i}$. For notational simplicity we continue to write $P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}$ for the price series fed to the models, with the convention that $t$ now indexes pre-averaged blocks rather than individual ticks.

\section{Pairs trading with the baseline}\label{sec:pairsbaseline}

This section defines the baseline strategy as a self-contained pairs-trading rule \parencite{gatev2006pairs,vidyamurthy2004pairs}. The regime-aware extensions of Section~\ref{sec:regimemodels} keep this rule's entry direction and only modify when it is allowed to trade.

\subsection{Cointegration spread}\label{sec:spread}

Two non-stationary log-price series $\log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,A}$ and $\log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}$ are cointegrated if some linear combination is stationary \parencite{engle1987cointegration}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spreaddef}
    S_t \;=\; \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,A} \;-\; \beta\,\log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}.
\end{equation}
The hedge ratio $\beta$ is estimated by the Engle--Granger two-step procedure: OLS regression of $\log P^{\mathrm{mid},\,A}$ on $\log P^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}$, with stationarity of the residuals verified by an Augmented Dickey--Fuller test \parencite{dickey1979distribution,said1984testing}. High-frequency FX is not globally cointegrated: $\beta$ drifts with liquidity, session structure, and pair-specific shocks. We therefore estimate $\beta_t$ by rolling OLS with window $W_\beta$. Writing $x_s = \log P_s^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}$, $y_s = \log P_s^{\mathrm{mid},\,A}$, $\mathcal{W}_t = \{t-W_\beta+1,\ldots,t\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rollingbeta}
    \beta_t \;=\; \frac{W_\beta \sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} x_s y_s - \bigl(\sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} x_s\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} y_s\bigr)}{W_\beta \sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} x_s^{2} - \bigl(\sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} x_s\bigr)^{2}},
    \qquad
    \alpha_t \;=\; \tfrac{1}{W_\beta}\!\Bigl(\sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} y_s - \beta_t \sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t} x_s\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The cointegration spread and the associated spread return are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rollingspreadret}
    S_t \;=\; \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,A} - \beta_t \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,B} - \alpha_t,
    \qquad
    \Delta S_t \;=\; r_t^{A} - \beta_{t-1}\, r_t^{B},
\end{equation}
with $r_t^{i} = \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i} - \log P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}$. The lag $\beta_{t-1}$ ensures the hedge ratio is observable when the return is realised.

\subsection{The rolling \texorpdfstring{$z$-score}{z-score} and the baseline rule}\label{sec:zscore}

The baseline reads $S_t$ through a rolling $z$-score over a window of length $W_z$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zscore}
    Z_t \;=\; \frac{S_t - \mu_t^{S}}{\sigma_t^{S}},
    \quad
    \mu_t^{S} = \tfrac{1}{W_z}\!\sum_{s=t-W_z+1}^{t}\! S_s,
    \quad
    (\sigma_t^{S})^{2} = \tfrac{1}{W_z}\!\sum_{s=t-W_z+1}^{t}\!\bigl(S_s - \mu_t^{S}\bigr)^{2}.
\end{equation}
With entry threshold $z_q$ and exit threshold $z_x$, the position $\pi_t \in \{-1,0,+1\}$ is updated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baselinerule}
    \pi_t \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
        +1 & \pi_{t-1}=0,\; Z_t < -z_q, \\
        -1 & \pi_{t-1}=0,\; Z_t > +z_q, \\
        \phantom{+}0 & \pi_{t-1} \neq 0,\; |Z_t| \leq z_x, \\
        \pi_{t-1} & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
A long-spread position is opened when $S_t$ is sufficiently below its rolling mean and closed when it returns toward zero; the short rule is symmetric \parencite{gatev2006pairs}. For every unit of leg $A$ we trade $\hat{\beta}$ units of leg $B$, so the resulting portfolio is exposed to the relative move between the two legs rather than to FX direction \parencite{vidyamurthy2004pairs}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{plots/long-short.pdf}
    \caption{Mechanics of pairs trading. The combined long/short portfolio is exposed only to the relative move between the two legs.}
    \label{fig:longshort}
\end{figure}

A do-nothing reference $\pi_t^{\mathrm{BH}} = +1$ for every $t$ (Buy \& Hold) earns $\Delta S_t$ at each step, pays no transaction costs, and provides a passive drift benchmark.

\subsection{Why the baseline is not enough}\label{sec:baselinelimits}

The baseline assumes $S_t$ is governed by a single stationary process, so that fixed $(z_q, z_x)$ correctly characterise ``far from equilibrium'' for every $t$. Two empirical features of FX tick data violate this assumption: (i) squared spread returns exhibit strong positive autocorrelation, consistent with conditional heteroskedasticity \parencite{engle1982arch,bollerslev1986garch,cont2001empirical}; and (ii) a spread that mean-reverts quickly in quiet periods may drift directionally for hundreds of bars during news-driven or one-sided-flow episodes \parencite{hasbrouck2007microstructure}. The fixed-threshold rule has no mechanism to distinguish these cases. This motivates the regime-aware extensions below.

\section{Regime-aware models}\label{sec:regimemodels}

We introduce the AR/HMM machinery on a generic series, then specialise to the spread $S_t$.

\subsection{Autoregressive benchmark}\label{sec:ar}

The autoregressive model of order $p$ projects a series $Y_t$ on its $p$ most recent past values \parencite{hamilton1994,tsay2010}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arp}
    Y_t \;=\; c \;+\; \phi_{1} Y_{t-1} \;+\; \cdots \;+\; \phi_{p} Y_{t-p} \;+\; \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}).
\end{equation}
The simplest non-trivial case is $\mathrm{AR}(1)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ar1}
    Y_t \;=\; c + \phi\, Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t,
\end{equation}
with unconditional mean $c/(1-\phi)$ for $|\phi|<1$. The $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ captures momentum ($\phi > 0$) or immediate mean reversion ($\phi < 0$), but only if $(c,\phi,\sigma^{2})$ are constant over the sample. We use \eqref{eq:ar1} as a building block, applied to $Y_t = S_t$ in Section~\ref{sec:msarspread}.

\subsection{Hidden Markov chain}\label{sec:hmm}

To relax the structural-stability assumption we let the parameters of \eqref{eq:ar1} be piecewise constant, switching with an unobserved discrete-time Markov chain $K_t \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ \parencite{hamilton1989new,rabiner1989tutorial,krolzig1997msvar,cappe2005}. The dynamics of $K_t$ are governed by a $K \times K$ transition matrix $\mathbf{P} = (p_{ij})$ and an initial distribution $\nu = (\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K)^{\top}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transitionmatrix}
    p_{ij} = \mathbb{P}(K_t = j \mid K_{t-1} = i),
    \qquad
    \sum_{j=1}^{K} p_{ij} = 1 \;\;\forall i,
    \qquad
    \nu_k = \mathbb{P}(K_1 = k).
\end{equation}
The Markov assumption is deliberately simple: the future depends on the past only through the current state. The framework is nonetheless rich enough to capture persistence, sudden switching, and regime-dependent parameters within a single probabilistic model \parencite{rabiner1989tutorial}.

\subsection{Markov-switching AR(1) on the spread}\label{sec:msarspread}

We specialise to $S_t$ from \eqref{eq:rollingspreadret} and take $K = 2$. The joint generative model is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:msargen}
    S_t \mid S_{t-1},\, K_t = k \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(c^{(k)} + \rho^{(k)} S_{t-1},\, (\sigma^{(k)})^{2}\bigr),
    \qquad
    \mathbf{P} = \begin{pmatrix} p_{11} & p_{12} \\ p_{21} & p_{22} \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
This is an $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ on the spread with all three parameters $(c^{(k)},\rho^{(k)},\sigma^{(k)})$ switching \parencite{hamilton1989new,krolzig1997msvar}. For $|\rho^{(k)}|<1$ the regime mean is $\mu^{(k)} = c^{(k)}/(1-\rho^{(k)})$, yielding the mean-deviation form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meandevparam}
    S_t \;=\; \mu^{(k)} + \rho^{(k)}\bigl(S_{t-1} - \mu^{(k)}\bigr) + \varepsilon_t^{(k)},
    \qquad \varepsilon_t^{(k)} \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(0,(\sigma^{(k)})^{2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Values of $\rho^{(k)}$ close to zero correspond to fast mean reversion; values close to unity, to near-random-walk behaviour. Before estimation, $S_t$ is winsorised at $\mathrm{median}(S) \pm 4\cdot\mathrm{std}(S)$ and rescaled by $10^{4}$ for numerical stability.

\subsection{Estimation by EM (Baum--Welch)}\label{sec:em}

The HMM parameter set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetadef}
    \Theta \;=\; \bigl\{\, \nu,\; \mathbf{P},\; c^{(k)},\; \rho^{(k)},\; \sigma^{(k)} : k=1,\ldots,K \,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
estimated by maximum likelihood with the Expectation--Maximisation algorithm \parencite{dempster1977}, which for HMMs is the Baum--Welch procedure \parencite{baum1970maximization,rabiner1989tutorial}. The E-step computes the smoothed regime posteriors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothedposterior}
    \gamma_t(k) \;=\; \mathbb{P}(K_t = k \mid S_1,\ldots,S_T;\Theta)
\end{equation}
via the forward--backward algorithm. The M-step updates $(c^{(k)},\rho^{(k)},\sigma^{(k)})$ and $\mathbf{P}$ to maximise the expected complete-data log-likelihood. The iteration is run to a small tolerance, restarted from several random seeds to mitigate convergence to local maxima \parencite{cappe2005}.

\subsection{Model selection}\label{sec:modelselection}

The number of states $K$ and AR order $p$ are chosen by information criteria \parencite{akaike1974aic,schwarz1978bic,esl,tsay2010}. With $m$ free parameters, $T$ observations, and maximised likelihood $\hat L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aicbic}
    \mathrm{AIC} \;=\; 2m - 2\ln\hat L, \qquad \mathrm{BIC} \;=\; m\ln T - 2\ln\hat L.
\end{equation}
The AIC targets predictive accuracy; the BIC penalises complexity more aggressively in large $T$ and favours parsimony. We sweep over a grid of $(K,p)$ and retain the configuration that minimises both criteria for the trading simulations.

\subsection{Regime classification}\label{sec:regimes}

With $K=2$, regimes are labelled by their innovation variance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regimelabels}
    k_{\mathrm{MR}} = \arg\min_{k}\sigma^{(k)},
    \qquad
    k_{\mathrm{DR}} = \arg\max_{k}\sigma^{(k)},
\end{equation}
giving a mean-reverting (MR) regime and a drifting (DR) regime. The smoothed posteriors are $\gamma_t^{\mathrm{MR}} = \mathbb{P}(K_t = k_{\mathrm{MR}} \mid S_1,\ldots,S_T)$ and $\gamma_t^{\mathrm{DR}} = 1 - \gamma_t^{\mathrm{MR}}$. In live trading, only past observations are available; the filtered posterior $\mathbb{P}(K_t = k_{\mathrm{MR}} \mid S_1,\ldots,S_t)$ is then used inside the walk-forward back-test, while the smoothed posterior is reserved for diagnostic figures.

\subsection{Regime-aware strategies}\label{sec:regimestrategies}

We build two regime-aware strategies on top of the baseline \eqref{eq:baselinerule}; both inherit its entry/exit directions and only change \emph{when} the rule is allowed to trade.

\textbf{AR-gated.} A binary gate with danger threshold $\delta \in (0,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:argate}
    G_t \;=\; \mathbbm{1}\!\bigl\{\gamma_t^{\mathrm{MR}} \geq 1-\delta\bigr\},
\end{equation}
modifies the rule to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arpositionrule}
    \pi_t \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
        0 & G_t = 0 \;\text{(liquidate, stay flat),}\\
        \pi_t^{\mathrm{baseline}} & G_t = 1.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The gate is binary: trade exactly like the baseline, or sit in cash. It also applies to existing positions: if we are long the spread and the HMM flags danger, we go flat at the next bar.

\textbf{MS-AR.} A continuous, regime-weighted entry threshold,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:msarthreshold}
    z_q^{\mathrm{eff}}(t) \;=\; \gamma_t^{\mathrm{MR}}\, z_q \;+\; \gamma_t^{\mathrm{DR}}\, z_v,
    \qquad z_v \geq z_q,
\end{equation}
replaces $z_q$ in \eqref{eq:baselinerule} by $z_q^{\mathrm{eff}}(t)$. When MR is highly probable, $z_q^{\mathrm{eff}} \approx z_q$ and the strategy trades like the baseline; when DR dominates, a larger $|Z_t|$ deviation is required before entering.

\section{Back-test protocol and metrics}\label{sec:backtest}

\subsection{Transaction costs and walk-forward evaluation}\label{sec:walkforward}

Every position change in leg $i$ pays the half-spread $\mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i}$ of \eqref{eq:midprice}, plus a fixed commission \parencite{aldridge2013hft,lopezdeprado2018advances}. The round-trip cost of one pair-position is therefore approximately $\mathrm{HS}^{A} + \hat\beta\,\mathrm{HS}^{B}$ in basis points. Strategies are evaluated in a walk-forward structure: parameters $\Theta$ and the hyperparameters $(W_\beta, W_z, z_q, z_x, z_v, \delta)$ are estimated on a training segment and applied to a disjoint out-of-sample test segment, with windows then slid forward \parencite{lopezdeprado2018advances,hautsch2012econometrics}.

\subsection{Performance metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

Let $R_t$ denote the strategy return at bar $t$, $\mu_R = \mathbb{E}[R]$, $\sigma_R = \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(R)}$, $V_t$ the portfolio value with running peak $V_t^{\star} = \max_{s \leq t} V_s$, and $R_f$ a benchmark risk-free rate (set to zero). Statistical metrics describe the shape of the return distribution; financial metrics translate it into capital outcomes \parencite{tsay2010,cont2001empirical}.
\begin{align}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{ann}} &= \sigma_R\sqrt{T},
    & \mathcal{S} &= \tfrac{\mathbb{E}[(R-\mu_R)^{3}]}{\sigma_R^{3}},
    & \mathcal{K} &= \tfrac{\mathbb{E}[(R-\mu_R)^{4}]}{\sigma_R^{4}}, \label{eq:statmetrics}\\
    \mathrm{VaR}_\alpha &= -Q_{1-\alpha}(R),
    & \mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha &= -\mathbb{E}[R \mid R \leq -\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha],
    & \mathrm{TF} &= \tfrac{\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha}{\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha}, \label{eq:varcvar}
\end{align}
with $\alpha = 0.95$ \parencite{artzner1999coherent}. The drawdown family uses $D_t = (V_t^{\star} - V_t)/V_t^{\star}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drawdown}
    \mathrm{MDD} = \max_t D_t,
    \qquad
    \mathrm{UI} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_t D_t^{2}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{UI}$ is the Ulcer index of \textcite{martin1989ulcer}; the maximum drawdown is treated in \textcite{magdonismail2004drawdown}. Risk-adjusted ratios relate excess return to risk:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratios}
    \mathrm{SR} = \tfrac{\mu_R - R_f}{\sigma_R}
    \quad\text{\parencite{sharpe1994ratio}},
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Sortino} = \tfrac{\mu_R - R_f}{\sigma_d}
    \quad\text{\parencite{sortino1991downside}},
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Calmar} = \tfrac{\mathrm{AR}}{\mathrm{MDD}}.
\end{equation}
Trade-level diagnostics (win rate, market exposure, profit factor, payoff ratio) summarise frequency and size; see Section~\ref{sec:result} for their interpretation.
