\section{Data and pre-processing}\label{sec:data}

I use high-frequency bid--ask FX tick data from \textcite{dukascopy}, loaded into Python with \texttt{pandas} \parencite{mckinney2010pandas} and \texttt{NumPy} \parencite{harris2020numpy}. Time-series and Markov-switching estimation use \texttt{statsmodels} \parencite{seabold2010statsmodels}; the inner back-test and forward--backward routines are JIT-compiled with \texttt{Numba} \parencite{lam2015numba} to make the per-day re-fit feasible on tick data. The full data-processing and modelling pipeline, including the back-test driver and the figures reproduced in this thesis, is released as the open-source repository \texttt{stk-mat2011} \parencite{furnes2026repo}. The two legs of a pair are indexed by $i \in \{A, B\}$. The three pair constructions are AUD/USD--NZD/USD, EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK, and GBP/USD--EUR/USD; they span a deliberate range of liquidity, transaction costs, and pair-dependence \parencite{gatev2006pairs,krauss2017statarb}.

Rather than running a single continuous back-test, I study three independent one-month sub-samples: August 2024, December 2024, and April 2025. These months were chosen because they cover periods of active price movement on all three pairs while spanning distinct macroeconomic backdrops (summer dollar weakness, year-end positioning, and the spring 2025 tariff-driven episode), which gives three structurally different episodes on which the same machinery can be exercised. Each month is fitted independently per trading day: the rolling cointegration $(\beta_t,\alpha_t)$, the $z$-score, and the Markov-switching AR(1) are all re-estimated from scratch every session, so any look-ahead in the smoothed regime posteriors is bounded by a single day.

Bid and ask streams are sorted by timestamp and aligned by a backward as-of merge \parencite{hasbrouck2007microstructure}. I write $P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i}$ and $P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i}$ for the prevailing bid and ask of leg $i$ at time $t$. The mid-price and basis-point half-spread are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:midprice}
\begin{aligned}
    P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i} \;&=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i} + P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i}\bigr),\\[3pt]
    \mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i} \;&=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i} - P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i}}{P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}}\,\times\, 10^{4}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i}$ approximates the cost of crossing the market for one leg \parencite{roll1984spread}; since pairs trading enters and exits both legs simultaneously, the combined spread cost is central to the back-test.

\paragraph{Pre-averaging on number of ticks.}\label{sec:preaveraging}
Tick data are dominated by microstructure noise (bid--ask bounce, discreteness, asynchronous quote updates) that contaminates the observed price relative to the latent efficient price \parencite{roll1984spread,hasbrouck2007microstructure,hautsch2012econometrics}. Following \textcite{jacod2017}, I apply a pre-averaging step in tick time: each block of $L$ consecutive ticks is collapsed into a single observation by averaging the mid-prices within the block, so that with $\{P_{t_n}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}\}_{n}$ denoting the synchronised tick-time mid-price for leg $i$, the pre-averaged series at block index $k$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preaverage}
    \widetilde P_k^{\,i} \;=\; \frac{1}{L}\sum_{n=(k-1)L+1}^{kL} P_{t_n}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i},
    \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots
\end{equation}
The block size $L$ is the same for both legs of a pair, so the pre-averaged legs remain perfectly synchronised. Pre-averaging is preferred here to fixed calendar-time sampling because it produces an observation index that is more nearly homogeneous in microstructure activity and substantially reduces the noise variance of the resulting return series \parencite{jacod2017,aitsahalia2014,andersen2000great}. All subsequent quantities (log returns, cointegration spread, $z$-score, and HMM observations) are computed on the pre-averaged series $\widetilde P_k^{\,i}$. For notational simplicity I continue to write $P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}$ for the price series fed to the models, with the convention that $t$ now indexes pre-averaged blocks rather than individual ticks.
