\section{Data and pre-processing}\label{sec:data}

We use high-frequency bid--ask FX tick data from \textcite{dukascopy}. The full data-processing and modelling pipeline, including the back-test driver and the figures reproduced in this thesis, is implemented in Python and released as the open-source repository \texttt{stk-mat2011}, available on GitHub \parencite{furnes2026repo}; implementation details, package versions, and reproduction scripts are documented there. The two legs of a pair are indexed by $i \in \{A, B\}$. The three pair constructions are AUD/USD--NZD/USD, EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK, and GBP/USD--EUR/USD; they span a deliberate range of liquidity, transaction costs, and pair-dependence \parencite{krauss2017statarb}.

Rather than running a single continuous back-test, we study three independent one-month sub-samples: August 2024, December 2024, and April 2025. These months cover periods of active price movement on all three pairs and span distinct macroeconomic backdrops (summer dollar weakness, year-end positioning, and the spring 2025 tariff-driven episode), giving three structurally different episodes on which the same machinery can be exercised. Each trading day inside a month is treated as its own self-contained back-test: every model and threshold introduced later (the rolling cointegration in Section~\ref{sec:spread}, the $z$-score in Section~\ref{sec:zscore}, and the regime model in Section~\ref{sec:msarspread}) is re-fitted from scratch at the start of each session, so any in-sample look-ahead is bounded by one day.

\paragraph{From bid and ask to mid and cost.}
Bid and ask streams are sorted by timestamp and aligned by a backward as-of merge. We use both quotes in two different roles. The \emph{mid-price} is the model input: the bid and ask are averaged into a single price that approximates the unobservable efficient price, and that mid-price is the only price the strategy sees when it decides whether to trade. The \emph{half-spread}, by contrast, is not fed to the model at all; it is held aside and used inside the back-test in Section~\ref{sec:walkforward} as the per-leg transaction cost paid whenever the strategy crosses the market. Writing $P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i}$ and $P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i}$ for the prevailing bid and ask of leg $i$ at time $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:midprice}
\begin{aligned}
    P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i} \;&=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i} + P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i}\bigr),\\[3pt]
    \mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i} \;&=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{P_t^{\mathrm{ask},\,i} - P_t^{\mathrm{bid},\,i}}{P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}}\,\times\, 10^{4}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{HS}_t^{\,i}$ is the basis-point cost of crossing the spread on one leg \parencite{roll1984spread}; since a pairs trade enters and exits both legs simultaneously, the combined per-flip cost $\mathrm{HS}_t^{\,A} + \hat\beta\,\mathrm{HS}_t^{\,B}$ is what determines whether the rolling rule of Section~\ref{sec:zscore} clears its friction.

\paragraph{Pre-averaging on number of ticks.}\label{sec:preaveraging}
The raw mid-price is contaminated by microstructure noise (bid--ask bounce, discreteness, asynchronous quote updates) relative to the latent efficient price \parencite{hasbrouck2007microstructure}. Following \textcite{jacod2017}, we pre-average in tick time: blocks of $L$ consecutive ticks are collapsed into one observation by averaging the mid-prices within the block. Letting $P_{\tau}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}$ denote the raw tick-level mid-price of leg $i$ at tick $\tau$, the pre-averaged mid-price at block index $t$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preaverage}
    \overline{P}_t^{\,i} \;=\; \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\tau=(t-1)L+1}^{tL} P_{\tau}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i},
    \qquad t = 1, 2, \ldots
\end{equation}
The block size $L$ is the same for both legs, so the pre-averaged legs remain perfectly synchronised. Pre-averaging is preferred to fixed calendar-time sampling because it produces an observation index that is more nearly homogeneous in microstructure activity and substantially reduces the noise variance of the resulting return series \parencite{jacod2017}. From this point onward, every quantity built from the mid-price (log-returns, cointegration spread, $z$-score, HMM observations) is built from the pre-averaged series $\overline{P}_t^{\,i}$, and $t$ indexes blocks rather than ticks. To keep the equations in later sections light, we write $P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i} \equiv \overline{P}_t^{\,i}$ in Sections~\ref{sec:pairsbaseline}--\ref{sec:result}.
