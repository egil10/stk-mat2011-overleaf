\section*{Abstract}

This thesis asks whether Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) add value to a classical pairs-trading rule on high-frequency currency tick data \parencite{gatev2006pairs,vidyamurthy2004pairs,avellaneda2010statarb,krauss2017statarb}. Bid--ask tick data from \textcite{dukascopy} for three currency-pair constructions---AUD/USD--NZD/USD, EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK, and GBP/USD--EUR/USD---are pre-averaged on number of ticks \parencite{jacod2017} and studied across three independent monthly episodes (August 2024, September 2024, and August 2025), chosen for active price movement under structurally different macro backdrops. The pre-averaged mid-price series is used to build a rolling cointegration spread \parencite{engle1987cointegration,dickey1979distribution}. The textbook $z$-score rule on this spread is the baseline strategy; a Markov-switching AR(1) on the same spread \parencite{hamilton1989new,krolzig1997msvar} is the regime model. The HMM is used as a regime gate \emph{on top of} the baseline, not as an independent forecaster: an AR-gated variant switches the baseline off in a high-volatility drifting regime, and an MS-AR variant widens the entry threshold continuously with the drifting-regime probability. All strategies are evaluated under a walk-forward back-test with realistic transaction costs \parencite{roll1984spread,lopezdeprado2018advances}. We find that the HMM functions as an interpretable defensive filter rather than as a source of additional risk-adjusted return: it correctly identifies high-variance episodes of the spread, but on the pairs where the baseline is already profitable, removing exposure during those episodes removes profitable exposure as well, and risk-adjusted ratios fall accordingly \parencite{sharpe1994ratio,sortino1991downside}.
