\section*{Abstract}

This thesis asks whether Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) add value to a classical pairs-trading rule on high-frequency currency tick data. Two years of bid--ask quote streams (2024--2025) from Dukascopy for three economically related currency-pair constructions---AUD/USD--NZD/USD, EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK, and GBP/USD--EUR/USD---are aggregated into volume bars and used to build a rolling cointegration spread for each pair. The textbook $z$-score pairs-trading rule on this spread serves as the baseline strategy; a Markov-switching AR(1) on the same spread serves as the regime model. The HMM is used as a regime gate on top of the baseline, giving rise to two extensions: a binary AR-gated rule that switches the baseline off in a high-volatility drifting regime, and a probability-weighted MS-AR rule that widens the entry threshold continuously with the inferred drifting-regime probability.

All three strategies are evaluated in a walk-forward back-test that includes the bid--ask half-spread and a fixed commission as transaction-cost frictions. The baseline rule is strongly profitable on AUD/USD--NZD/USD (Sharpe~$6.88$), mildly profitable on GBP/USD--EUR/USD (Sharpe~$2.19$), and uniformly unprofitable on EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK, which exhibits weak rolling cointegration and wide spreads. Adding the HMM regime gate sharply reduces market exposure and annual volatility on every pair, but reduces total return and risk-adjusted ratios (Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar) more than it reduces drawdown on the two profitable pairs. On the unprofitable pair the gate reduces the size of the loss but does not convert the strategy into a profitable one. The MS-AR variant produces a smoothed version of the binary AR gate and underperforms it on risk-adjusted measures.

The thesis therefore concludes that, in this sample and under realistic transaction costs, Hidden Markov regime filters function as interpretable defensive devices rather than as sources of additional risk-adjusted return: they correctly identify high-variance episodes of the spread, but the profitable opportunities are not concentrated in the low-variance regime to a degree sufficient to compensate for the loss of exposure that the gate imposes.
