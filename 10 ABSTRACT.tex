\section*{Abstract}

This thesis asks whether Hidden Markov Models can improve a textbook pairs-trading rule on high-frequency FX tick data. The baseline is a standard $z$-score rule on a rolling cointegration spread between two related currency pairs. The regime model is a two-state Markov-switching AR(1) fitted to the same spread. We do not use the HMM as a separate forecaster. We use it only as a gate that switches the baseline off when the regime looks unfavourable. The trading direction is therefore the same as the baseline, and the only thing the HMM changes is when the rule is allowed to trade.

We compare four strategies on the same data: passive Buy~\&~Hold, the baseline, an AR-gated variant, and a continuous MS-AR threshold. We run them on three currency-pair constructions across three one-month episodes, with a realistic cost model that charges the half-spread plus a small commission on every flip.

The baseline loses money on every pair--month: it trades too often, and the small per-trade alpha does not cover the cost. The HMM gate cuts trading activity and losses sharply, but the strategy never becomes profitable. The continuous MS-AR threshold does not beat the simpler binary gate. Passive Buy~\&~Hold has the highest Sharpe ratio on most cells. The negative result is best read as a statement about the $z$-score rule under realistic FX costs: in this setting the HMM works as a defensive filter, not as a source of new return.
