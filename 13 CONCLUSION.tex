\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis tested whether Hidden Markov Models can add value to a classical pairs-trading rule on high-frequency FX tick data. The set-up is layered: the baseline is a textbook rolling $z$-score on a cointegration spread, and the HMM is used only as a regime gate that can switch the same baseline off. Four strategies — passive Buy~\&~Hold, the baseline, an AR-gated variant, and a continuous MS-AR threshold — were compared on the same nine pair--month cells under a realistic cost model with per-day refits.

At a high level: the cointegration baseline is unprofitable on every pair--month at realistic costs; the HMM gate cuts exposure and cumulative loss sharply but never makes the strategy profitable; the continuous MS-AR variant does not improve on the simpler binary gate; and passive Buy~\&~Hold beats the three active strategies on Sharpe in most cells. The regime model itself is well behaved, so the negative result is not driven by a misbehaving fit. It is driven by the cost-to-alpha ratio of the underlying $z$-score rule.

The exercise has clear limits. We use three pair constructions, three one-month episodes, $K = 2$ regimes, unit-size positions, a cost model that ignores latency and market impact, and an in-sample-smoothed gating posterior. The three episodes were also chosen for active price movement, which biases the comparison towards months where a passive long-spread position can drift directionally. The conclusion should therefore be read as a statement about the textbook $z$-score rule under realistic FX costs, not about HMMs as a class.

Three extensions follow naturally. First, pair the regime posteriors with an entry signal that is itself profitable after costs (a momentum signal in the drifting regime is the obvious candidate), rather than asking the HMM to rescue a mean-reversion signal that is already cost-dominated. Second, replace the per-day smoothed posterior with a strict walk-forward filter that uses only past observations; since this weakens the gate, the negative conclusion only tightens. Third, an HMM--GARCH hybrid that lets within-regime variance evolve smoothly is a natural fit for the fat tails seen in the spread returns. The full implementation, daily ledgers, and per-pair tearsheets are released as the open-source repository \texttt{stk-mat2011} as a starting point for follow-up work.
