\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis has tested whether Hidden Markov Models add value to a classical pairs-trading rule on high-frequency FX tick data. The exercise was deliberately layered: a single baseline signal (the rolling $z$-score of the cointegration spread \parencite{engle1987cointegration,gatev2006pairs,vidyamurthy2004pairs}) was first treated as a self-contained strategy and back-tested under realistic transaction costs and a walk-forward protocol \parencite{aldridge2013hft,lopezdeprado2018advances}; the HMM was then introduced as a regime gate \parencite{hamilton1989new,rabiner1989tutorial,krolzig1997msvar}, with two concrete instantiations (a binary AR-gated rule and a probability-weighted MS-AR threshold) that leave the baseline's directional entry rule unchanged.

The findings are summarised as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The baseline rule separates the three pairs cleanly.} AUD/USD--NZD/USD is strongly profitable, GBP/USD--EUR/USD is mildly profitable, and EUR/NOK--EUR/SEK is unprofitable. The baseline back-test is itself a diagnostic for whether a pair is tradeable under high-frequency frictions \parencite{krauss2017statarb}.
    \item \textbf{The HMM is a defensive filter, not a return enhancer.} The regime gate sharply reduces market exposure and annual volatility on every pair. On the two profitable pairs, however, it removes profitable exposure as well as dangerous exposure, and risk-adjusted ratios fall \parencite{sharpe1994ratio,sortino1991downside}. On the unprofitable pair, the HMM only limits the damage.
    \item \textbf{The MS-AR threshold smoothing does not improve on the binary AR gate} on a risk-adjusted basis.
    \item \textbf{The model is interpretable.} The smoothed regime posteriors, regime-specific innovation variances, and regime-conditional return distributions are consistent with financial intuition for a low-volatility mean-reverting regime and a high-volatility drifting regime \parencite{cappe2005}.
\end{enumerate}

The central contribution is not a claim that HMMs universally outperform simpler models, but a careful, costed, walk-forward test of when regime awareness helps, when it fails, and how mathematical models behave when confronted with noisy, costly, non-stationary tick data \parencite{cont2001empirical,hautsch2012econometrics}.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The exercise is restricted to three pair constructions, three independent one-month sub-samples (August 2024, September 2024, and August 2025), $K=2$ regimes, unit-size long/flat/short positions, and a half-spread-plus-commission cost model that omits latency and market impact.

\paragraph{Future directions.} The most natural extension is to combine the regime posteriors with a different entry signal in the drifting regime---for example, a momentum signal as in \textcite{avellaneda2010statarb}---rather than using the HMM only as a gate on a mean-reversion signal. A second direction is to pre-screen the pair universe with the baseline back-test before applying the HMM, avoiding modelling effort on pairs that the microstructure does not support \parencite{krauss2017statarb}.
