\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis has tested whether Hidden Markov Models add value to a classical pairs-trading rule on high-frequency FX tick data. The exercise was deliberately layered: a single baseline signal (the rolling $z$-score of the cointegration spread \parencite{engle1987cointegration,gatev2006pairs,vidyamurthy2004pairs}) was first treated as a self-contained strategy and back-tested under realistic transaction costs and a per-day fit \parencite{aldridge2013hft,lopezdeprado2018advances}; the HMM was then introduced as a regime gate \parencite{hamilton1989new,rabiner1989tutorial,krolzig1997msvar}, with two concrete instantiations (a binary AR-gated rule and a probability-weighted MS-AR threshold) that leave the baseline's directional entry rule unchanged.

The findings are summarised as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The cointegration baseline is unprofitable at realistic frictions.} Across all three pair constructions and all three monthly episodes (August 2024, December 2024, April 2025), the rolling $z$-score rule generates a stream of round-trips at small per-trade alpha and bleeds net of the half-spread plus commission \parencite{krauss2017statarb}. This is the binding constraint of the entire exercise: there is no signal here that an HMM can rescue.
    \item \textbf{The HMM gate is a defensive filter, not a return enhancer.} Both regime-aware strategies sharply reduce market exposure (typically from $\sim 30\%$ for the Baseline to $5$--$15\%$ for AR and $6$--$17\%$ for MS-AR), the number of round-trips (by a factor of three to five), and annual volatility. The corresponding loss is roughly $75$--$85\%$ smaller than for the Baseline on every pair-month, but never crosses into profit \parencite{sharpe1994ratio,sortino1991downside}.
    \item \textbf{MS-AR does not improve on the binary AR gate.} The continuous, regime-weighted threshold of \eqref{eq:msarthreshold} produces equity curves that are nearly indistinguishable from---and occasionally slightly worse than---the binary danger gate of \eqref{eq:argate}. The extra parameter $z_v$ fails to justify itself at this cost level.
    \item \textbf{The model is interpretable.} The smoothed regime posteriors, regime-specific innovation variances, and regime-conditional return distributions are consistent with the textbook two-regime picture of a low-volatility mean-reverting state and a high-volatility drifting state \parencite{cappe2005}, both visually and in the per-day fit summaries.
\end{enumerate}

The central contribution is not a claim that HMMs universally outperform simpler models---they do not---but a careful, costed, per-day-fit test of when regime awareness helps, when it fails, and how mathematical models behave when confronted with noisy, costly, non-stationary tick data \parencite{cont2001empirical,hautsch2012econometrics}.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The exercise is restricted to three pair constructions, three independent one-month sub-samples (August 2024, December 2024, and April 2025), $K=2$ regimes fitted day by day, unit-size long/flat/short positions, a half-spread-plus-commission cost model that omits latency and market impact, and an in-sample smoothed posterior for the AR/MS-AR gates (the walk-forward forward filter is deferred to follow-up work). The negative finding on profitability is therefore best read as a statement about the textbook cointegration $z$-score rule under realistic FX frictions, not about HMMs as such.

\paragraph{Future directions.} The most natural extension is to combine the regime posteriors with a different entry signal in the drifting regime---for example, a momentum signal as in \textcite{avellaneda2010statarb}---rather than using the HMM only as a gate on a mean-reversion signal. A second direction is to pre-screen the pair universe with the baseline back-test before applying the HMM, avoiding modelling effort on pairs that the microstructure does not support \parencite{krauss2017statarb}. A third is to replace the per-day in-sample smoothing with a true walk-forward filter, which can only lower the value of the regime gate further and is therefore the conservative direction in which to push the negative result.
