\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis tested whether Hidden Markov Models add value to a classical pairs-trading rule on high-frequency FX tick data. The set-up is deliberately layered: the baseline is a textbook rolling $z$-score on a cointegration spread, and the HMM is used only as a regime gate that can switch the same baseline off. Four strategies (passive Buy~\&~Hold, baseline, an AR-gated variant, and a continuous MS-AR threshold) were compared on the same nine pair--month cells under a realistic half-spread plus commission cost model with per-day refits.

At a high level, the cointegration baseline is unprofitable on every pair--month at realistic frictions, the HMM gate cuts exposure and cumulative loss substantially but never restores profitability, the continuous MS-AR variant does not improve on the binary gate, and passive Buy~\&~Hold dominates the three active strategies on a Sharpe-ratio basis in most cells. The regime model itself is well behaved, so the negative result on profitability is not driven by a misbehaving fit but by the cost-to-alpha ratio of the underlying $z$-score rule.

The exercise has clear limits: three pair constructions, three one-month episodes, $K = 2$ regimes, unit-size positions, a cost model that omits latency and market impact, and an in-sample-smoothed gating posterior. The three episodes were also chosen for active price movement, which biases the comparison toward periods in which a passive long-spread position can drift directionally. The conclusion should therefore be read as a statement about the textbook $z$-score rule under realistic FX frictions, not about HMMs as a class.

Three extensions follow naturally. First, pair the regime posteriors with an entry signal that is itself profitable after costs, for instance a momentum signal in the drifting regime, rather than asking the HMM to rescue a mean-reversion signal that is already cost-dominated. Second, replace the per-day smoothed posterior by a strict walk-forward filter that uses only past observations; since this weakens the gate, the negative conclusion tightens. Third, an HMM--GARCH hybrid that lets within-regime variance evolve smoothly is a natural fit for the fat tails observed in the spread returns. The full implementation, daily ledgers, and per-pair tearsheets are released as the open-source repository \texttt{stk-mat2011} as a starting point for follow-up work.
