\section{Pairs trading with the baseline}\label{sec:pairsbaseline}

This section defines the baseline strategy as a self-contained pairs-trading rule \parencite{gatev2006pairs}. The regime-aware extensions of Section~\ref{sec:regimemodels} inherit its entry direction and only change when it is allowed to trade.

\paragraph{Cointegration spread.}\label{sec:spread}
Two non-stationary log-price series $\log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,A}$ and $\log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}$ are cointegrated if some linear combination of them is stationary \parencite{engle1987cointegration}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spreaddef}
    S_t \;=\; \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,A} \;-\; \beta\,\log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}.
\end{equation}
The hedge ratio $\beta$ is fitted by the Engle--Granger two-step method: an OLS regression of $\log P^{\mathrm{mid},\,A}$ on $\log P^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}$, with the residuals tested for stationarity using an Augmented Dickey--Fuller test \parencite{dickey1979distribution}. High-frequency FX is not globally cointegrated: $\beta$ drifts with liquidity, time of day, and pair-specific shocks. We therefore estimate a rolling $\beta_t$ over a window of $W_\beta$ bars. Writing $x_s = \log P_s^{\mathrm{mid},\,B}$, $y_s = \log P_s^{\mathrm{mid},\,A}$, and $\mathcal{W}_t = \{t-W_\beta+1,\ldots,t\}$ with window means $\bar x_t, \bar y_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rollingbeta}
    \beta_t \;=\; \frac{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t}(x_s - \bar x_t)(y_s - \bar y_t)}{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{W}_t}(x_s - \bar x_t)^{2}},
    \qquad
    \alpha_t \;=\; \bar y_t \;-\; \beta_t\, \bar x_t.
\end{equation}
The cointegration spread and its bar-by-bar return are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rollingspreadret}
\begin{aligned}
    S_t \;&=\; \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,A} \;-\; \beta_t \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,B} \;-\; \alpha_t,\\[2pt]
    \Delta S_t \;&=\; r_t^{A} \;-\; \beta_{t-1}\, r_t^{B},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with bar log-returns $r_t^{i} = \log P_t^{\mathrm{mid},\,i} - \log P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{mid},\,i}$. The lag $\beta_{t-1}$ in the return makes sure the hedge ratio used to weight the legs is already known when the return is realised.

\paragraph{Rolling $z$-score and the baseline rule.}\label{sec:zscore}
The baseline reads $S_t$ through a rolling $z$-score over a window of length $W_z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zscore}
    Z_t \;=\; \frac{S_t - \mu_t^{S}}{\sigma_t^{S}},
\end{equation}
with rolling mean and variance
\begin{equation*}
    \mu_t^{S} \;=\; \frac{1}{W_z}\sum_{s=t-W_z+1}^{t} S_s,
    \qquad
    \bigl(\sigma_t^{S}\bigr)^{2} \;=\; \frac{1}{W_z}\sum_{s=t-W_z+1}^{t}\bigl(S_s - \mu_t^{S}\bigr)^{2}.
\end{equation*}
With entry threshold $z_q$ and exit threshold $z_x$, the position $\pi_t \in \{-1,0,+1\}$ updates by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baselinerule}
    \pi_t \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
        +1 & \pi_{t-1}=0,\; Z_t < -z_q, \\
        -1 & \pi_{t-1}=0,\; Z_t > +z_q, \\
        \phantom{+}0 & \pi_{t-1} \neq 0,\; |Z_t| \leq z_x, \\
        \pi_{t-1} & \text{otherwise.}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
A long-spread position is opened when $S_t$ is far enough below its rolling mean and closed once it comes back; the short rule is the mirror image. For every unit of leg $A$ we trade $\hat{\beta}$ units of leg $B$, so the portfolio is exposed to the relative move between the two legs rather than to FX direction \parencite{vidyamurthy2004pairs}. As a benchmark we also track a do-nothing reference that simply holds a long unit spread position throughout the sample, $\pi_t^{\mathrm{BH}} = +1$ for every $t$ (Buy~\&~Hold). It is the simplest possible answer to ``what would happen if we did not trade at all?'' It earns the bar-by-bar move $\Delta S_t$ of the cointegration spread, pays no transaction costs, and isolates the directional drift of the spread itself from any active timing. Any active rule that cannot beat this passive line on a risk-adjusted basis is, on the data we look at, not earning its costs.

The baseline assumes that $S_t$ is generated by a single stationary process, so that one fixed pair of thresholds $(z_q, z_x)$ captures ``far from equilibrium'' at every $t$. Two well-known features of FX tick data make this assumption fragile: the variance of $\Delta S_t$ clusters in time (conditional heteroskedasticity, \parencite{cont2001empirical}), and the spread can drift directionally over hundreds of bars during news-driven episodes \parencite{hasbrouck2007microstructure}. The fixed-threshold rule cannot tell these episodes apart from ordinary mean reversion, which motivates the regime-aware extensions of Section~\ref{sec:regimemodels}.
